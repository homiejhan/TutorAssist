Problem 1. **Undoing operations in reverse order**

[box: Key idea]
A *two-step equation* has two operations on the variable. To solve it, undo them in the reverse of the order they were done ("last done, first undone"):
(1) Undo the addition or subtraction.
(2) Undo the multiplication or division.
(3) Check by substituting your answer into the original equation.
[/box]

> **Vocabulary.** The *coefficient* is the number multiplying the variable; the *constant* is the number standing alone.

**Example A.** Solve $3x + 5 = 20$.
Step 1. Subtract $5$ from both sides:
  $$3x = 15$$
Step 2. Divide both sides by $3$:
  $$x = 5$$
Step 3. Check: $3(5) + 5 = 15 + 5 = 20$ $\checkmark$

[box red: Common mistake]
Dividing first: $\frac{3x + 5}{3}$ is **not** $x + 5$ — the $5$ must be divided by $3$ too. Undo the $+5$ first.
[/box]

**Now you try.**
(a) Solve $4x - 7 = 21$.
(b) Solve $\frac{x}{2} + 3 = 11$.

Problem 2. **Equations with a negative coefficient**

## Watch the sign
- A negative coefficient stays attached to the variable when you divide.
- Dividing both sides by a negative number flips each sign.

**Example B.** Solve $-2x + 9 = 1$. \\
Subtract $9$: $-2x = -8$. \\
Divide by $-2$: $x = 4$.

---

**Now you try.**
(a) Solve $-5x + 2 = 27$.
[space large]
(b) Explain in one sentence why you subtract before you divide.
[lines 3]
[workspace none]

Answer Key
1. (a) $4x = 28$, so $x = 7$.
(b) $\frac{x}{2} = 8$, so $x = 16$.
2. (a) $-5x = 25$, so $x = -5$.
(b) Subtraction was done last to $x$ (after multiplying), so it is undone first.
